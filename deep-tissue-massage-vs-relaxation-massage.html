Deep Tissue vs Relaxation Massage

Two popular massage experiences, two different approaches. Here's how deep tissue and relaxation massage differ and how to decide which may suit you.

When searching for a massage in Lagos, you may come across several different treatment options. Two of the most common are relaxation massage and deep tissue massage.

Although both involve massage techniques, they can feel quite different. Understanding the distinction can help you communicate your preferences and choose an experience that is appropriate for you.

RELAXATION

Relaxation Massage

Generally focuses on a calm, comfortable experience with techniques and pressure intended to promote relaxation.

FOCUSED

Deep Tissue Massage

Uses slower and more focused pressure to work on deeper layers of muscle and connective tissue.

What is relaxation massage?

Relaxation massage is generally chosen by people who want to unwind and enjoy a calming massage experience. Pressure is typically adjusted according to the client's preference and comfort.

What is deep tissue massage?

Deep tissue massage is a more focused form of massage that uses slower movements and firmer pressure to target deeper layers of muscle and connective tissue.

It can feel more intense than a relaxation massage, which is why communication with your therapist is particularly important.

Which massage is better for relaxation?

If your primary goal is simply to unwind and enjoy a calming experience, relaxation massage is generally the more natural choice.

Which massage should I choose for muscle tension?

People who want more focused attention around areas of muscular tension may prefer deep tissue massage. However, the appropriate treatment depends on the individual and the nature of the discomfort.

If you have persistent, severe or unexplained pain, massage should not replace appropriate medical assessment.

Is deep tissue massage supposed to hurt?

Deep tissue massage can feel more intense than relaxation massage, but discomfort should not simply be ignored. Communicate with your therapist throughout the treatment so pressure can be adjusted when necessary.
