The Lagos workplace problem

What is corporate ergonomics?

Corporate ergonomics is the practical process of arranging workstations, equipment, movement habits and workplace practices so that people can perform their work more comfortably and sustainably. In a Lagos office environment, this should include not only the desk but also the prolonged sitting and commuting that can surround the workday.

Why this matters

Ergonomics is more than buying an office chair

A healthier workplace approach looks at the entire pattern of work rather than one piece of equipment.

For many professionals in Lagos, the working day does not begin when they sit at their desk. A person may spend a significant period sitting in traffic, arrive at work and remain at a workstation for several hours, then return home through another long commute.

That makes movement, workstation arrangement and recovery relevant parts of workplace wellness. The goal is not to create one perfect sitting position that every employee must maintain all day. The more useful objective is to create a workstation that supports the employee and a work culture that allows regular changes of position and movement.

Corporate ergonomics can therefore involve monitor height, seating position, desk arrangement, keyboard and mouse placement, foot support, movement breaks, workstation assessments and employee education.

A practical principle

The objective is not “perfect posture” for eight hours. The objective is a workstation that supports the employee while encouraging regular changes of position and movement.

Workstation setup

A practical ergonomic sitting position

These are useful starting points for a workstation review, not rigid rules that apply identically to every person.

Area Practical starting point What to watch for
Eyes & monitor Position the monitor so that the top third is around eye level and place the screen at a comfortable viewing distance. Around 20–30 inches (approximately 50–75 cm) is a useful starting range for many desktop setups. Persistent downward viewing, leaning toward the screen or repeatedly bending the neck.
Back & spine Use the chair backrest and lumbar support to support the natural shape of the lower back. A slightly reclined position, often around 100–110 degrees, can be a useful starting point. Slouching, collapsing forward or sitting without adequate back support.
Elbows & arms Keep the shoulders relaxed and position the elbows comfortably, often around 90–100 degrees, with the forearms broadly parallel to the desk when using a keyboard. Raised shoulders, reaching too far forward or keeping the wrists in uncomfortable positions.
Hips & knees Aim for a comfortable seated position with the hips and knees roughly around right angles, adjusting the chair to the person rather than forcing a fixed angle. Perching on the edge of the chair, excessively high or low seating, or restricted leg movement.
Feet Keep the feet comfortably supported on the floor or use an appropriate footrest where necessary. Feet dangling, repeatedly tucking the feet underneath the chair or remaining in one constrained position for long periods.
Important:

Ergonomic numbers are starting points, not universal medical prescriptions. Body size, workstation design, visual requirements, existing conditions and job tasks can change what is appropriate. Where an employee has persistent or significant symptoms, an appropriately qualified healthcare or occupational-health professional should be consulted.

Common workplace hazards

Three patterns worth correcting

south

Slouching and forward-head posture

When a monitor, laptop or phone is poorly positioned, employees may repeatedly move their head and upper body forward to see the screen. A better setup can reduce the need for sustained forward positioning.

event_seat

Perched-edge sitting

Sitting on the front edge of a chair for extended periods can make it harder to use the backrest effectively. Employees should be able to sit in a supported position and change position when needed.

pause_circle

Static stagnation

Even a well-arranged workstation does not remove the need to move. Remaining in one position for long periods is a useful signal to introduce a short movement interruption.

Movement at work

Build micro-breaks into the workday

Small movement interruptions can be easier for organisations to maintain than a wellness programme that depends on employees finding a large block of free time.

A simple hourly movement routine

  • check Stand up. Change from the seated position where your work allows.
  • check Open the chest. Gently move the shoulders and upper body instead of remaining fixed at the keyboard.
  • check Move the spine. Use comfortable standing or seated movement rather than holding one posture continuously.
  • check Move the legs. A few standing calf raises or a short walk can break up prolonged sitting.

A practical 2–3 minute reset

Where the workplace permits it, employees can use approximately two to three minutes each hour for a brief movement reset.

  1. 1 Stand and breathe comfortably.
  2. 2 Perform gentle chest-opening movements.
  3. 3 Add a comfortable spinal movement.
  4. 4 Walk briefly before returning to the workstation.

Beyond the workstation

What about the Lagos commute?

A corporate ergonomics strategy that ignores commuting misses an important part of the employee's daily routine.

Before the workday

Long periods of sitting during a commute can mean that an employee begins the workday after already spending substantial time in a seated position.

Employers cannot control the entire commute, but they can recognise it when designing realistic wellness education.

After the workday

The return journey can create another extended period of sitting after hours at a desk.

A simple recovery routine involving comfortable movement, walking and changes of position can be a useful part of an employee's evening routine.

Corporate wellness insight

For Lagos-based teams, wellness planning can be more realistic when it recognises the complete workday: commute → workstation → meetings → workstation → commute → home.

Employee checklist

Five-minute workstation review

Use this as a practical starting checklist when reviewing your desk.

  • check Is your monitor positioned so you are not constantly looking down?
  • check Can your back use the chair's support rather than forcing you to perch forward?
  • check Are your shoulders relaxed while using the keyboard and mouse?
  • check Are your feet comfortably supported?
  • check Can you change position and stand periodically during your work?

Questions for HR or management

  • check Do employees know how to adjust their workstation?
  • check Does the organisation encourage reasonable movement breaks?
  • check Are employees able to report workstation concerns?
  • check Is ergonomic education included in workplace wellness planning?
  • check Does the wellness strategy include recovery beyond the desk?

Frequently asked questions

Corporate ergonomics FAQs

Straightforward answers to common workplace ergonomics questions.

What is corporate ergonomics?

Corporate ergonomics is the practical process of arranging workstations, equipment, movement habits and workplace practices around the needs of the people performing the work.

Why does ergonomics matter for Lagos office workers?

Many Lagos professionals combine prolonged commuting with long periods of desk work. Recognising the complete workday can help organisations create more realistic workplace wellness practices.

How high should my computer monitor be?

A useful starting point is to position the top third of the monitor around eye level and place the screen at a comfortable viewing distance. Individual needs vary, so the setup should be adjusted to the person and task.

How often should employees move during desk work?

A practical approach is to interrupt prolonged sitting regularly. For example, a two- to three-minute movement break approximately once an hour can be used where the job allows it.

Can massage fix poor workplace ergonomics?

Massage does not correct the underlying design of a workstation. It can be part of a broader wellness and recovery strategy, while ergonomic assessment and workplace changes address the working environment itself.

When should an employee seek professional care?

Persistent, severe, worsening or otherwise concerning symptoms should not simply be managed through workplace wellness activities. An appropriately qualified healthcare professional should assess symptoms when medical or physiotherapy care may be required.

Can Pax & Pearl support a corporate wellness programme?
