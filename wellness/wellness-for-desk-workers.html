Desk Worker Wellness

Desk Worker Wellness in Lagos: A Practical Guide for Back, Neck, Shoulder & Stress

Spending much of the working day at a desk can make movement, posture, recovery and stress management easy to overlook. This guide provides practical habits for professionals who work extensively with computers and desks.

Written and professionally reviewed by Gift Hassel, Nigerian certified Wellness and Massage Professional.
Last reviewed: 14 September 2026

Quick answer

Long periods of desk work can contribute to stiffness, muscular tension, physical fatigue and discomfort for some people. Practical strategies include changing position regularly, taking movement breaks, improving workstation setup, building recovery into the day and paying attention to persistent symptoms.

Massage may help some people relax and manage everyday muscular tension, but it is not a diagnosis or universal treatment for pain. Significant, persistent, worsening or unexplained symptoms should be assessed by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.

Why can desk work leave your body feeling uncomfortable?

Desk work often requires the body to remain in relatively similar positions for extended periods. At the same time, attention is concentrated on screens, documents, meetings and other tasks.

The issue is not that sitting itself is automatically harmful or that there is one perfect posture that everyone must maintain all day. A more useful principle is to avoid remaining in one position for too long and to make regular movement part of the working day.

Common areas desk workers notice tension

Neck

Neck stiffness or discomfort may be noticed after prolonged computer work, particularly when the head and upper body remain relatively static.

Shoulders

Shoulders can feel tight after long periods of keyboard, mouse and screen use, especially during stressful work.

Upper back

The upper back may feel stiff or tired after extended desk sessions with limited movement.

Lower back

Some people experience lower-back discomfort when sitting for long periods or when their workstation does not support comfortable positioning.

What is a better sitting position for office work?

There is no single posture that is perfect for every person. Instead, aim for a comfortable workstation that allows you to change position and move regularly.

  • Keep your screen at a comfortable viewing height.
  • Position the keyboard and mouse so you do not have to reach excessively.
  • Allow your shoulders to remain relatively relaxed rather than continuously elevated.
  • Use a chair that provides comfortable support.
  • Keep your feet supported where practical.
  • Avoid treating one fixed sitting position as something you must maintain for hours.
  • Change position and move throughout the day.

The goal is not to sit rigidly. The goal is to make the workstation comfortable enough that movement and position changes are practical.

The desk-worker movement principle

Instead of waiting until the end of the day to “exercise away” eight or more hours of inactivity, build small movement opportunities into the working day.

A simple desk-day checklist

  • Stand or change position periodically.
  • Walk briefly when there is a natural opportunity.
  • Use breaks to move rather than remaining at the same workstation.
  • Relax your shoulders and avoid unnecessary tension.
  • Look away from your screen periodically.
  • Drink water regularly according to your individual needs.
  • Make time for adequate rest outside working hours.

A practical 5-minute desk reset

A short movement reset can be useful during a long workday. Keep the movements gentle and comfortable rather than forcing a painful range of motion.

  1. Stand up and take a few relaxed breaths.
  2. Walk around the workspace briefly.
  3. Move your shoulders gently through a comfortable range.
  4. Change your neck position gently without forcing it.
  5. Return to your workstation and adjust your position if necessary.

If a particular movement causes pain, stop it rather than trying to push through the symptom.

How often should office workers take movement breaks?

There is no universal break schedule that works for every job. A practical approach is to avoid allowing long uninterrupted periods of desk work to become the default.

Use natural transitions—finishing a task, ending a call, getting water, going to another part of the office or taking a scheduled break—as opportunities to change position and move.

Companies can also build simple movement prompts into their workplace culture rather than relying entirely on employees to remember.

Why can shoulders feel tight after a long workday?

Shoulder tension can have several possible contributors, including sustained positioning, repetitive computer tasks, stress and general muscular fatigue. The exact cause varies from person to person.

If shoulder discomfort is persistent or accompanied by other concerning symptoms, it should not automatically be assumed to be ordinary desk-related tension.

Can massage help desk workers?

Massage may help some people with relaxation and the temporary experience of muscular tension. For a desk worker, the appeal may be less about “fixing posture” and more about creating an opportunity for physical relaxation and recovery.

Massage should be approached according to the individual's needs, comfort and circumstances.

Relaxation massage vs deep tissue massage for desk workers

Approach Potential focus Important consideration
Relaxation massage Relaxation, comfort and general recovery. Often appropriate when the primary goal is a calming wellness experience.
Deep tissue massage More focused work on areas of muscular tension. Pressure should remain appropriate and comfortable for the individual.

Neither option should be selected solely from a website article when someone has an unexplained medical condition or significant pain. Professional assessment may be more appropriate in those circumstances.

Should you get a massage after sitting at a desk all week?

Some desk workers choose massage as part of their personal recovery routine after demanding work periods. Whether it is useful for you depends on your goals, preferences, health circumstances and how your body responds.

Massage works best as one part of a broader routine that includes movement, rest and sensible work habits.

What about stress?

Desk work is not purely a physical experience. Deadlines, workload, difficult conversations, customer demands, financial responsibilities and constant notifications can all contribute to mental strain.

A useful recovery routine therefore needs to include psychological decompression as well as physical movement.

  • Create a clear transition between work and personal time.
  • Take genuine breaks rather than simply switching from one screen to another.
  • Use relaxation activities that work for you.
  • Protect adequate sleep and recovery time.
  • Discuss unreasonable workload or workplace concerns through appropriate channels.

The end-of-day desk-worker reset

Step 1 — Close work

Finish the workday deliberately rather than allowing unfinished tasks to continuously occupy your attention.

Step 2 — Move

Give yourself an opportunity to walk, stretch gently or otherwise change position.

Step 3 — Decompress

Create some distance from work-related notifications and mental demands.

Step 4 — Recover

Prioritise rest, sleep and activities that help you recover before the next demanding day.

When desk-related discomfort deserves medical attention

Do not assume that every neck, shoulder or back symptom is caused by sitting at a desk.

Seek appropriate healthcare assessment for severe, persistent, worsening or unexplained symptoms, or when symptoms are associated with other concerning changes.

Massage and wellness services should not be used to delay needed medical evaluation.

Desk-worker wellness for Lagos professionals

The realities of desk work can vary considerably across Lagos. Employees may work in offices, hybrid arrangements, home offices, co-working environments or businesses where desk work is only part of the day.

The useful principle remains the same: create opportunities to move, work comfortably, recover properly and recognise when a symptom needs professional healthcare attention.

For employers, these individual habits can become part of a broader workplace wellness strategy.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my back hurt after sitting at work all day?

Prolonged static positioning, workstation setup, muscular fatigue and other factors may contribute to discomfort. Persistent or concerning pain should be appropriately assessed.

How does sitting for long periods affect office workers?

Long periods with little movement can contribute to stiffness, physical fatigue and discomfort for some people. Regular movement and position changes are practical ways to reduce prolonged static time.

What is the best sitting position for office workers?

There is no single perfect position for everyone. Aim for a comfortable workstation and change position regularly instead of remaining rigidly in one position for hours.

Can massage help with neck and shoulder tension?

Massage may help some people relax and experience temporary relief from muscular tension, but persistent or unexplained symptoms should be assessed appropriately.

How often should a desk worker get a massage?

There is no universal schedule. Frequency should depend on personal preference, goals, response to massage and relevant health circumstances.

Is stretching better than massage?

They serve different purposes and are not necessarily competing alternatives. Regular movement and appropriate stretching can be part of daily habits, while massage can provide a separate relaxation or recovery experience.

What should I do if desk work causes severe pain?

Do not assume the symptom is simply workplace tension. Severe, persistent, worsening or unexplained symptoms should be assessed by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.
