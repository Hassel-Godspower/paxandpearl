Workplace Wellness in Lagos: A Practical Guide for Employees and Employers

Workplace wellness is more than a company gym, an occasional wellness event or a motivational message. It is the practical effort to help people support their physical wellbeing, recovery, comfort, stress management and healthy routines while they work.

Written and reviewed by: Gift Hassel Credential: Nigerian Certified Wellness and Massage Professional Last reviewed: September 12, 2026

Quick Answer

Workplace wellness is the collection of practical habits, workplace practices, education and supportive activities that help employees maintain their wellbeing while managing the demands of work.

For office-based employees, this can include regular movement, sensible workstation habits, recovery time, stress-management practices, appropriate wellness education and access to activities that support relaxation and physical comfort. Employers and HR teams can support this through thoughtful workplace policies, wellness programmes, staff activities and a culture that takes employee wellbeing seriously.

What Is Workplace Wellness?

Workplace wellness refers to practical efforts that support employee wellbeing within the context of working life. It recognises that the way people work can affect how they feel physically, mentally and emotionally.

A person who spends most of the day sitting at a computer may experience a very different set of challenges from someone whose job requires constant movement. A banker, software professional, administrator, call-centre worker, executive or entrepreneur may spend many hours sitting, looking at screens, concentrating intensely or dealing with demanding schedules.

Workplace wellness therefore needs to be practical. It should consider the real working environment rather than treating wellness as a one-time event.

A useful way to think about workplace wellness:

Help people work sustainably, recover appropriately and develop practical habits that support their wellbeing.

Why Does Workplace Wellness Matter?

Work occupies a significant part of many people's lives. For professionals who spend long hours at desks, the workplace can influence daily movement, posture, stress, rest patterns and opportunities for recovery.

A workplace wellness approach does not mean that an employer is responsible for every aspect of an employee's health. Rather, it means creating reasonable opportunities and supportive practices that encourage employees to care for themselves.

For employees, this may mean having access to wellness education, movement opportunities, appropriate breaks and activities that encourage relaxation and recovery.

For employers, it can mean thinking deliberately about the conditions in which employees work and the ways the organisation can encourage healthier routines.

Workplace Wellness for Desk Workers

Desk-based work deserves particular attention because it can involve long periods of sitting and screen use.

A desk worker may spend several hours concentrating on a computer without noticing how little they have moved. Over time, remaining in one position for too long can contribute to physical discomfort or stiffness for some people.

Practical workplace wellness can therefore include:

  • Taking appropriate movement breaks during the workday.
  • Paying attention to comfortable workstation setup.
  • Avoiding unnecessarily long periods in one position.
  • Including simple movement or stretching in the workday.
  • Paying attention to persistent discomfort instead of simply ignoring it.
  • Making time for appropriate recovery outside work.

Physical Wellbeing at Work

Physical wellbeing at work is not simply about exercise. It includes the everyday physical demands of a person's working environment.

For office workers, this may involve prolonged sitting, repetitive computer use, screen time and limited movement. For other professions, it may involve standing for long periods, repetitive tasks or physically demanding work.

A useful workplace wellness programme should therefore consider what employees actually do throughout the day.

Movement

Encouraging employees to change position and move periodically can be a simple part of a healthier working routine.

Workstation habits

Employees can benefit from understanding how their chair, desk, screen and working position affect comfort.

Recovery

Recovery is often overlooked. People may finish a demanding workday and immediately move into another set of responsibilities without allowing themselves meaningful time to unwind.

Stress, Relaxation and Recovery

Workplace wellness should also recognise the difference between working hard and never allowing the body and mind to recover.

Employees may experience demanding deadlines, customer interactions, long meetings, financial responsibilities, performance expectations or extended screen time.

Wellness strategies can include practical approaches to breaks, relaxation, movement, sleep, social connection and personal recovery.

Employers can support this by building a culture where reasonable breaks and healthy routines are not treated as signs of poor commitment.

Where Can Massage Fit Into Workplace Wellness?

Massage can be one component of a broader wellness programme. It should not be presented as a replacement for medical care or as a solution to every workplace health problem.

For some people, professional massage can be part of their personal relaxation and recovery routine. Employees who spend long hours sitting may also choose massage as one option for addressing general muscular tension and relaxation.

Depending on the individual's circumstances and goals, different massage approaches may be considered, including relaxation-focused or deeper-pressure massage.

What Can Employers Do to Support Employee Wellness?

Workplace wellness does not have to begin with an expensive programme. Organisations can start with practical changes that make healthy behaviour easier.

  • Encourage appropriate movement and break practices.
  • Provide useful wellness education.
  • Pay attention to workstation comfort.
  • Create opportunities for staff wellness activities.
  • Encourage employees to take reasonable recovery time.
  • Listen to employees about workplace wellbeing concerns.
  • Consider professional wellness providers for appropriate staff programmes.
  • Make wellness activities voluntary and accessible.

A good programme should fit the organisation rather than simply copying what another company does.

The Role of HR in Employee Wellness

Human Resources teams are often in a unique position to coordinate employee wellness because HR sits at the intersection of people, policies and organisational culture.

HR teams can begin by asking practical questions:

  1. What are the most common wellbeing concerns employees report?
  2. What does a typical workday look like for employees?
  3. Are employees getting opportunities to move and recover?
  4. What wellness activities would employees actually use?
  5. How can wellness activities be made voluntary and inclusive?
  6. How will the organisation evaluate participation and feedback?

Corporate Wellness in Lagos

Lagos workplaces include banks, professional services firms, technology companies, schools, retail businesses, healthcare organisations, government offices, entrepreneurs and many other types of employers.

Their wellness needs are not identical.

A useful corporate wellness programme should therefore begin with the people and working environment involved.

Depending on the organisation, activities could include wellness education, movement sessions, staff appreciation activities, relaxation initiatives, workplace wellness days or professional massage services.

Wellness for Professionals

Different professional groups may face different patterns of work.

Bankers, for example, may experience long periods of computer work, demanding schedules, customer-facing responsibilities and periods of intense concentration.

Other professionals may work remotely, run businesses, manage teams or spend long hours travelling between locations.

The principle remains the same: wellness strategies should respond to the actual demands of the person's work.

What Makes a Good Workplace Wellness Programme?

A useful programme should be practical, understandable and relevant to employees.

It should not simply consist of occasional activities that employees are expected to attend without understanding why they are useful.

A stronger programme considers:

  • Employee needs.
  • Workplace realities.
  • Accessibility.
  • Voluntary participation.
  • Useful education.
  • Opportunities for movement and recovery.
  • Feedback.
  • Consistency.

When Should Workplace Discomfort Be Taken Seriously?

Not every ache or period of tiredness requires medical attention. However, employees should not automatically assume that persistent or severe symptoms are simply a normal part of working.

Symptoms that are persistent, severe, worsening, unexplained or associated with other concerning signs should be evaluated by an appropriate healthcare professional.

Workplace wellness should complement appropriate healthcare, not compete with it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is workplace wellness?

Workplace wellness is the practical support, education, habits and activities that help employees care for their wellbeing within the context of work.

What is the difference between employee wellness and corporate wellness?

Employee wellness focuses on supporting individual employees, while corporate wellness generally describes the broader organisational approach to wellbeing.

Can massage be part of a workplace wellness programme?

Yes. Professional massage can be included as one component of a broader wellness programme, particularly where the goal is relaxation and general recovery. It should not be presented as a replacement for medical care.

Is workplace wellness only for office workers?

No. Workplace wellness applies across different occupations. However, the appropriate approach should reflect the actual physical and psychological demands of each job.

What can HR teams do first?

HR can begin by understanding employee needs, reviewing the realities of the work environment and choosing practical, voluntary wellness activities that employees are likely to find useful.
